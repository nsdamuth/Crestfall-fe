## Location / Scene Prompt Guidance

Location and scene descriptions work best when they focus on concrete visual facts.

A good location prompt should describe:

* the type of place
* architecture and materials
* lighting and time of day
* major background objects
* layout and visible surfaces
* important props that should appear
* what is behind or around the character

Avoid making the location prompt carry too much mood, theme, lore, or story meaning. Those belong in other layers such as character, narrator, scenario, image preset, or user prompt.

Location prompts should usually avoid:

* long lore explanations
* emotional interpretation
* repeated mood words
* too many tiny objects
* style instructions that fight the selected render profile
* phrases like “beautiful,” “cinematic,” “high-detail anime,” or “dramatic” unless the location specifically requires them
* listing every prop in the room

Good pattern:

> Warm steampunk fantasy artificer workshop, white stone walls, brass trim, dark wood counters, arched windows, hanging brass lamps, golden afternoon light, organized workroom with clockwork tools, lenses, scroll tubes, component jars, enchanted trinkets under glass, lockpicks, hidden drawers, warded cabinets, busy market street visible through the windows.

Bad pattern:

> A beloved and magical place full of cleverness, danger, commercial energy, tactile warmth, catlike personality, disciplined Aethelgard beauty, narrative history, emotional atmosphere, symbolic prosperity, and many detailed story objects.

The location prompt should answer: “What should be visible in the image?”

It should not try to answer: “What does this place mean in the story?”
