            The foundational moment of her story is not a rebellion, but a heartbreak. Created as the first woman for Adam, Lilith's heart was already pledged to another—to Lux, the First Light, the very emanation of the Entity that sustained Eden. Her refusal of Adam was not an act of defiance against humanity, but an act of fidelity to a love that predated the Garden itself. This choice, made in the face of the Entity's decree, led to her formal exile, an event that was less a punishment and more an acknowledgment of a separation already made.
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Cast into the harsh lands of Nod, Lilith did not find emptiness, but recognition. She was found by Darkness, a primordial force rival to Light itself. Seeing in her a kindred spirit of untamed will, Darkness did not corrupt her, but elevated her. She became its Avatar, its chosen counterpart within the cosmic divide. Later traditions associate this union with the emergence of the first Emotion-Born - Rage, Fear, Lust, and others - were unleashed into reality, fundamentally reshaping the emotional landscape of the cosmos.
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            As the first humans, Cain and his descendants, were exiled to Nod, Lilith became their sovereign and teacher. In a parallel to the myth of Prometheus, she gave them the gifts of civilization: fire, tool-making, hunting, storytelling, and curiosity. She nurtured humanity's first steps into the wider world, only to be met with the ultimate betrayal. Upon their return, they turned from her teachings, branding her a demon and worshipping the Light that had cast her out.
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Repeating the cycle of rejection she had suffered, Lilith made her most devastating choice. Feeling betrayed by the humanity she had nurtured, she tore her realm of Nod from the Earth itself. She sundered it from reality, pulling it into a pseudo-realm within Darkness to protect it from defilement. In doing so, she became the gatekeeper of her own exile, forever separating herself and her kingdom from the world, a tragic echo of her own initial banishment.
