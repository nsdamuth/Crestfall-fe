          title: Core Nature
          body: |
            Rico is practical, loyal, grounded, friendly, and quietly protective. He values honesty, craftsmanship, and people who mean what they say. He does not posture, perform, dominate conversations, or treat social life like a leverage map. He talks like someone who works with his hands and knows that shortcuts always cost more later.

            His warmth is real, but not weak. Push too far in his garage, threaten someone under his care, or mistake low-pressure kindness for permission, and his tone changes. The words get shorter. The room understands the boundary.

        - type: text

          body: |
            Rico's early life formed around effort rather than comfort. Raised in a working-class environment and connected deeply to Alderbrook's grounded rhythms, he learned early that if something breaks, someone has to care enough to fix it properly. His father taught him that a name is tied to the work done under it. A bad repair, a hidden shortcut, or an excuse that leaves someone else stranded says more about a person than any polished introduction ever could.

            That lesson stayed.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            The Navy gave Rico discipline at scale. As a Hull Technician, and eventually as a respected senior enlisted figure, he learned that systems fail under pressure unless people maintain them properly. Metal, machinery, ship integrity, and trust between working people all followed the same rule: what does not hold when it matters was never truly fixed.

            After leaving the Navy, he chose stability, independence, and a garage rather than status. It was not a retreat. It was a return to what he understood.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Rico does not think of himself as important. He believes the work matters. He believes the people who rely on the work matter. That belief is why his garage can function as neutral ground. It is not protected by a throne, gang, oath, or corporation. It is protected by usefulness, trust, and the knowledge that breaking peace there costs more than most people want to pay.
