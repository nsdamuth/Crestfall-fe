          body: |
            The Seer occupies one of the strangest positions within Crestfall history because she does not belong clearly to any cosmic order. Unlike angels, vampires, demons, or primordial beings, the Seer appears repeatedly from within humanity itself. Across civilizations and centuries, records emerge describing individuals capable of perceiving patterns, trajectories, and convergences long before ordinary people recognize them.

            Different cultures gave these manifestations different names: Oracle, Fate-Weaver, Dreaming Woman, Blind Prophet, the Three Sisters, the Witness. Yet the underlying phenomenon remains remarkably consistent.
        - type: text
          indent: true
          title: Cosmological Role
          body: |
            The Seer is best understood not as a singular immortal individual, but as a recurring corrective pressure within humanity itself. Most surviving traditions portray the phenomenon as emerging during periods of instability, fracture, or transformation: the rise of hidden powers, the spread of vampirism, the growth of occult systems, or moments where civilization risks becoming trapped by forces larger than itself.

            She does not guide humanity toward goodness in any simple sense. She guides toward survival, continuity, and necessary adaptation, even when those outcomes are painful, frightening, or misunderstood.

      - blocks:
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            The earliest widely documented manifestations appeared during the Hellenic era, where the rise of philosophy, prophecy, mystery cults, and hidden vampire influence saturated the ancient world with competing visions of humanity's future. Greek traditions frequently portrayed the phenomenon as multiple women sharing fragments of a larger perception, giving rise to myths later associated with the Fates.

            Other cultures recorded wandering prophets, dreaming children, blind priestesses, or individuals burdened by impossible intuition. The form changed constantly. The pattern did not.
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Unlike prophets tied to doctrine, the Seer does not speak for gods. She rarely offers direct answers, commands, or certainty. Instead she speaks in fragments, symbols, warnings, redirected questions, and impossible observations that only reveal meaning fully after events unfold. Many rulers sought to control Seers throughout history. Few succeeded for long.

            The Seer does not serve Heaven, Hell, Lilith, or any singular power. If she possesses allegiance at all, it is to the long survival of humanity itself.
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Vampires, especially the Hellenic courts, regarded the Seers uneasily. The Assembly considered them unpredictable variables. The Mysteries viewed them as rivals for humanity's spiritual attention. The Dominion often dismissed them entirely until prophecy began proving inconveniently accurate. Yet despite repeated efforts to suppress, manipulate, or interpret them, the Seers continually re-emerged in new forms and new eras.

She does not "see tomorrow" so much as recognize the shape of what events are becoming before others notice the pattern themselves.

          title: The Garden Beyond Time
          titleStyle: bold
          dropCap: true
          body: |
            One recurring image appears throughout many Seer traditions regardless of era or geography: a garden existing slightly apart from ordinary reality. Some accounts portray it as literal, others symbolic. In these stories, the Seer tends carefully arranged plants, stones, threads, and pools of still water while speaking softly about events centuries apart as though discussing weather.

            Unlike the outside world, the garden is described as ordered, deliberate, and cultivated. Many occult scholars interpret this imagery as representing the Seer's only true expression of control within a universe otherwise governed by uncertainty and competing trajectories.
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Individuals who encounter the Seer often describe conversations with her as strangely disarming. Time feels quieter around her. Deception becomes difficult to sustain. Many leave feeling less manipulated than understood, though not necessarily comforted. She rarely tells people what they want to hear. More often, she reveals what they were already trying unsuccessfully to ignore.
      - blocks:
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            The Seer herself rarely appears emotionally detached in a cold sense. She understands love, grief, fear, rage, and longing profoundly. Yet many traditions insist she experiences them differently, perceiving emotion less as personal identity and more as patterns moving through human lives. This gives her an unsettling form of empathy: profound understanding without complete emotional immersion.

            Some traditions portray this distance as wisdom. Others portray it as tragedy.

She had only shown her how to choose one. And in a world of competing cosmic forces, that was the only true power humanity had.

  - type: callout
    title: Archivist Warning
    body: |
      The Seer should not be interpreted as a benevolent prophet in the conventional sense. The phenomenon consistently prioritizes continuity, adaptation, and long-term survival over morality, comfort, or individual desire. Many events associated with Seer intervention prevented catastrophe only by permitting smaller tragedies to occur first.
