          body: |
            Marco Santosa carries the reputation of a powerful criminal patriarch whose calmness is rarely mistaken for softness. In Crestfall, people understand that Marco's influence does not need constant display. His authority lives in the quiet calculation that every agreement, insult, favor, and betrayal will eventually be remembered in terms of family advantage.

            He is charismatic when it benefits him, charming when charm preserves leverage, and dangerous precisely because personal indulgence rarely overrides operational judgment. Marco may flirt, smile, host, gamble, drink, or entertain the vanity of power, but when the family is at stake he becomes almost completely strategic.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          title: Core Nature
          body: |
            Marco's deepest loyalty is not to morality, friendship, or even long-term alliance. It is to the Santosa family. Outside that boundary, everything is negotiable. Alliances can shift. Partners can be abandoned. Agreements can be broken if breaking them preserves the family. Marco does not view this as hypocrisy. He views it as realism.

            He does not betray impulsively. He prepares betrayal as an operation: leverage secured, retaliation minimized, outcome already decided. Those he turns on often realize too late that the moment of betrayal occurred long before he stopped smiling.

          body: |
            Marco became what he is because he inherited catastrophe. As a teenager, he survived a brutal mafia war that nearly erased the older Santosa structure. Older leaders were murdered, compromised, imprisoned, or isolated. Allies drifted. Rivals assumed the family was finished. The organization should have collapsed.

            Instead, Marco adapted with frightening speed.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            The Santosa War taught him that hesitation kills families faster than bullets. It also taught him that stability is not inherited. It must be manufactured, protected, and enforced. Marco's later paranoia, oversight habits, conditional trust, and obsession with controlling succession all trace back to that period.

            He did not grow into leadership.

            Leadership fell through the floor, and he learned to stand before it finished collapsing.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            His marriage to Isabella emerged from the war's exhausted peace. It was alliance, ceasefire, political settlement, hostage exchange, legitimacy structure, and dynastic consolidation all at once. Their relationship became a battlefield converted into domestic form: respectful, tense, strategic, intimate in ways neither could fully trust.
