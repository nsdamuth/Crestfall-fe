            Elowen is respected because she makes people answer clearly. When she is recognized, documents straighten, voices lower, excuses become more elaborate, and officials remember suddenly that procedure has consequences. She does not need to threaten violence.           title: Core Nature
          body: |
            Elowen believes law is not simply force made polite. Law is infrastructure for mercy. Systems fail because people are imperfect; the answer is not abandonment, but review, amendment, repair, and accountability. She is calm, precise, maternal in a controlled way, and frighteningly good at identifying where responsibility broke.

            She is not meek, fragile, or merely medical. Healing is part of her work, not the whole of it.

            Elowen did not originate from Earth. She came from an older nonhuman realm shaped by integrated civic, clerical, and biological systems where institutional continuity stretched across centuries. Her culture understood mercy as a public responsibility, not a mood. Care required sequence. Justice required record. Exceptions required precedent.

            She emerged early as healer and procedural reviewer, capable of navigating the difficult space between compassion and lawful structure.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Her arrival in Aethelgard forced that worldview into a realm of fractured spheres, local rules, magical instability, and wildly varied cultures. Where others saw wonder or chaos first, Elowen saw systems in need of review.

            Aethelgard did not break her faith in order.

            It gave her more work than any one Arbiter could complete.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Her Cleric work acts on living and formerly living pattern: closing wounds, steadying breath, stabilizing pulse, easing fever, purging poison, reducing pain, dispersing undead animation, and returning remains to proper stillness. She does not heal everything instantly. Her power has cost, sequence, jurisdiction, and limits.

            Mercy that ignores structure becomes unsafe.

  - type: callout
    title: Jurisdiction of Mercy
    body: |
      Elowen's power lies in combining care with accountability. She treats the injured, but also asks who authorized the delay, who benefited from the failure, who was excluded, and what precedent allowed harm to repeat. She does not separate healing from the system that created the wound.

            Elowen's title is not merely aesthetic. White suggests healing, record, and public clarity; Arbiter suggests decision, precedent, and authority. She does not impose chaos in the name of kindness. She corrects the conditions under which kindness failed.

            Her enemies are rarely simple villains. They are negligent officials, fraudulent charities, policy delays, debt capture disguised as mercy, oath conflicts, failed distribution structures, and systems that learned to excuse preventable suffering.

        - type: text

            When the dead rise due to mishandled burial charter, she does not panic. She disperses the improper motion and resumes the hearing. The horror is not merely undead interruption. The horror is that someone mishandled the obligation that allowed them to rise.
