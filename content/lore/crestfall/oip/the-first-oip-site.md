          titleStyle: bold
          dropCap: true
          body: |
            The federal government's awareness of Crestfall began accidentally during the expanding wartime intelligence operations of the 1940s. Initial reports arrived through fragmented channels: contradictory infrastructure surveys, impossible underground architecture, recurring psychological incidents among engineering crews, unexplained disappearances tied to restricted utility zones, and military-adjacent personnel describing spaces beneath the city that did not remain physically consistent between inspections.

            Most reports were dismissed immediately.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Enough anomalies accumulated, however, that portions of the wartime intelligence apparatus eventually concluded Crestfall represented something statistically impossible rather than merely bureaucratically disorganized. Early investigators did not understand the Wound, the Tear, or the hidden history surrounding the city.

            They believed they had discovered an unidentified environmental or psychological phenomenon.

        - type: image
          src: images/the-discovery.png

      - blocks:
        - type: text
          title: The Wartime Response
          indent: true
          body: |
            The emerging wartime intelligence structure responded according to its own institutional instincts: secrecy, compartmentalization, observation, and containment. A small classified site was established above one of the most accessible secondary pressure points connected to the Tear beneath Crestfall. Officially, the facility existed under mundane infrastructure and engineering authority.

            Unofficially, it became the first precursor to what would eventually evolve into OIP.

        - type: inline-quote
          text: The government did not discover a monster. It discovered a contradiction it could not categorize.
          attribution: reconstructed OIP fragment

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            The early facility was primitive by later standards. Researchers, engineers, military observers, psychologists, and intelligence officers attempted to interpret the Tear through the frameworks available to them: radiation exposure, stress disorders, electromagnetic interference, enemy sabotage, experimental psychology, spatial disorientation, mass suggestion, or undiscovered geological phenomena.

            Every explanation failed partially.

        - type: divider
          variant: ornate

        - type: text
          title: The Learning Curve
          indent: true
          body: |
            The government's understanding evolved rapidly but unevenly. The phenomenon beneath Crestfall resisted stable interpretation because it did not behave according to consistent scientific categories. Investigators made constant mistakes: assuming patterns where none existed, overlooking real anomalies because they resembled ordinary error, exposing personnel too deeply too quickly, misclassifying events, and repeatedly underestimating how strongly the Tear affected perception itself.

            The facility became a laboratory for institutional confusion.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Yet the researchers were not incompetent. Many were extraordinarily intelligent people attempting sincerely to study something fundamentally outside the assumptions of modern scientific reality. Over time, they adapted pragmatically. The facility shifted away from trying to explain the Tear completely and toward documenting recurring patterns, minimizing exposure damage, and controlling public visibility.

            Observation replaced certainty.

  - type: callout
    title: The First Modern Mistake
    body: |
      The first OIP site represents the beginning of the modern state's relationship with the supernatural: not mastery, but procedural adaptation to uncertainty. The government never fully understood the Tear. Instead, it learned how to survive near it operationally while accepting that complete comprehension might be impossible.

          title: The Obscurity Effect
          titleStyle: bold
          dropCap: true
          body: |
            One of the earliest discoveries surrounding the Tear disturbed investigators profoundly: the phenomenon itself appeared to resist sustained public attention naturally. Records became misplaced. Witness testimony contradicted itself. Journalists lost interest unexpectedly. Political oversight drifted elsewhere. Administrative pathways obscured themselves through ordinary bureaucratic friction at statistically impossible rates.

            The facility became difficult to notice institutionally.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Later OIP researchers would eventually describe this loosely as an obscurity effect: the deeper systems surrounding the Tear subtly encouraged fragmentation, distraction, compartmentalization, and informational drift rather than direct concealment. The phenomenon did not erase evidence.

            It made certainty difficult to maintain long enough for action.

      - blocks:
        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            This accidental obscurity allowed the precursor organization to survive through decades of government restructuring, intelligence reorganization, classified experimentation, Cold War paranoia, and programs later associated loosely with projects such as MKUltra or Stargate. Most officials interacting with the facility understood only fragments of its purpose at any given time.

            No one possessed the full picture.

            And beneath the first hidden facility under Crestfall, the Tear quietly studied the modern world learning how governments, institutions, bureaucracy, and human systems behaved when forced to observe something they could neither fully prove nor fully deny.

It learned how to continue functioning while standing beside it.
