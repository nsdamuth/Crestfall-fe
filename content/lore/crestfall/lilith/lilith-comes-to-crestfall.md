            For centuries following the collapse of the older immortal age, Lilith rarely involved herself directly in the evolving structures of human civilization. Kingdoms rose and fell too quickly. Empires blurred together. Mortal history accelerated beyond the scale of intervention she once practiced openly. Her attention increasingly focused elsewhere: the endless pressure between Light and Darkness, the movements of the emotion-born, and the deeper fractures spreading across reality itself.

            Yet Crestfall eventually drew even her attention.

        - type: text

Elizabeth remained one of the few surviving daughters from Lilith's older world-shaping periods whose existence still carried emotional and historical weight for her. The long silence surrounding Crestfall, Las Dueñas, and the strange equilibrium developing around the city gradually became difficult to ignore.

            Something important was happening there.

          indent: true
          body: |
            Lilith entered Crestfall quietly near the closing decades of the twentieth century. No apocalyptic manifestation accompanied her arrival. No war erupted. No hidden factions even recognized her presence fully at first. Modern civilization had become too dense, too distracted, and too psychologically fragmented for mythic arrivals to function the way they once had.

            The city absorbed her almost naturally.

        - type: text

            Most hidden actors within Crestfall never realized she had come at all. OIP lacked the conceptual framework to identify her correctly. The Santosas interpreted scattered disturbances as ordinary underworld instability. The city itself treated her like another impossible shadow moving through the noise of modern life.

            Only two figures recognized the truth immediately.

            The Seer.

            And Dalethia.

            Dalethia understood instantly what Lilith's presence represented. Their earlier encounter centuries before had already transformed the trajectory of her existence permanently. Now the same ancient force stood quietly inside the city Dalethia herself had spent centuries shaping above the Wound.

            Neither woman sought open conflict.

        - type: text
          indent: true
          body: |
            Lilith arrived expecting perhaps another hidden immortal domain, another lingering remnant of the older vampire world. Instead, she found something stranger: a modern city partially symbiotic with an ancient fracture in reality, hidden immortals who had adapted into infrastructure and systems rather than kingdoms, a government agency learning how to survive uncertainty operationally, and humanity reshaping the world faster than many immortal powers now could.

            Crestfall impressed her reluctantly.

            Elizabeth's reunion with Lilith carried strange emotional weight even by immortal standards. Centuries had passed since their earlier connection during the old world. Entire civilizations disappeared between those moments. Yet Elizabeth remained fundamentally recognizable to Lilith in ways modern humanity increasingly was not.

            Enduring.

            Still standing.

They no longer behaved like servants, children, or extensions of older immortal hierarchies. Crestfall had changed them. Modernity had changed them. They had become their own ecosystem.

            Lilith found this unsettling.

            And quietly admirable.
